# Reflection #5

Pod Members: **Esme, Frida, Melanie, Reyna**

## Reflection Questions

* How was the pacing of the capstone project? (i.e too slow, just right, too fast)

Overall, the pacing felt fast but manageable. At the beginning, we felt like we were advancing quickly, and by the end we really appreciated that momentum because it gave us enough time to tighten everything up. That extra polishing time helped us improve the user experience, strengthen reliability, and deliver something we are genuinely proud of.

* To what extent did your plan change over the course of development? Knowing that you know now, what would you do differently if you were starting over?

Our overall plan did not change drastically over the course of development. The bigger challenge was not what to build, but learning how to implement our ideas effectively and in the right order. If we were starting over, we would keep the same core scope, but we would front-load implementation spikes earlier for higher-risk features so we could reduce debugging pressure later in the sprint.

* How helpful were the labs and weekly assignments in preparing you to create a capstone project? Be specific, what topics do you still have questions about that may or may not have been covered?

The labs and weekly assignments were very helpful and directly transferable to our capstone work. We were able to reuse multiple patterns and techniques from those exercises in our real implementation, which sped up development and gave us more confidence in our technical decisions. We still have some follow-up questions around advanced production hardening and long-term scaling practices, but the labs gave us a strong practical foundation.

* When planning for the capstone project, which resources were the most helpful? (i.e mentors, instructors & TAs, ideation process, pod syncs, wireframes, sprint planning, bug bash, practice demo day, etc.)

The most helpful resources were pod syncs, bug bash, mentors, and instructors/TAs. Getting end-to-end feedback from other teams was especially valuable because it revealed usability gaps we could not always see ourselves. That outside perspective helped us prioritize the right fixes, improve our flow, and make the web app stronger before final delivery.
